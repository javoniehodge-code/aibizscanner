What should be corrected first?

1. Correct the missing pool and conflicting pet policy.

Where: The hotel roundup and the affected booking listing.

Why it matters for AI: These details can determine whether the hotel belongs in a recommendation at all.

2. Align the charges and inclusions.

Where: The booking listing’s mandatory-fee breakdown and breakfast description; the Charleston guide’s parking information.

Why it matters for AI: The same stay should not appear cheaper or include more benefits depending on which source AI finds.

3. Clarify the details that cannot yet be confirmed.

Where: The hotel’s room accessibility information and rate-specific cancellation terms, followed by the relevant listings.

Why it matters for AI: A general “accessible” or “free cancellation” label does not establish the conditions needed for a suitable recommendation.

How we judge consistency and freshness

Compare like with like: The same property, room, rate, package and policy period. Different packages are not automatically a contradiction.

Separate agreement from currency: Sources may agree while all repeating old information. When current validity cannot be established, say so.

Show evidence: A real report includes exact pages, relevant wording, check dates and the basis for calling information outdated. A retrieval date alone does not prove the fact is current.

Keep the denominator honest: Ten applicable decision facts in this example. Inapplicable items are excluded; unreadable sources are disclosed and never treated as missing content.

Handle changing rates separately: General price positioning and fee policies can be compared. A price for specific travel dates requires a fresh quote.
