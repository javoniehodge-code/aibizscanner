Section 2 · Clear & complete

Does your website give AI enough detail to recommend the right coffee?

Being able to find a product is the first step. Here we assess whether its information is clear, concise and complete enough for AI to match it to a customer’s needs.

AI can identify the products. Some recommendation decisions still require guesswork.

In this example, the website provides enough detail for 4 of 8 recommendation questions. It leaves gaps in flavor matching, brewing guidance, caffeine choices and value comparisons.

Scope: 8 recommendation questions across the same 6 selected pages.

We assessed the Home, All coffee products, Classic espresso-style ground coffee, Medium-roast ground coffee, Nespresso Original Line capsules and Frequently asked questions pages.

These are our checks of the website’s content, not four separate AI opinions. A gap means the necessary detail was not found clearly enough in these selected pages. It does not mean the product lacks that feature, or that no other page explains it.

How did we choose the questions?

Start with the decisions AI needs to make when recommending coffee: format, roast, flavor, brewing method, machine fit, where to buy, caffeine preferences and value. Use a consistent coffee checklist, and assess each question only for the products where it applies.

A plumber’s checklist would instead cover services, service areas, opening hours, emergency availability, fees and booking. This is a baseline assessment, not a test of whether a customer prompt happens to mention the brand.

What would give AI a stronger basis to recommend you?

1. State specific product differences in a short, factual summary.

Give each relevant product direct descriptions of roast, flavor, format and suitable brewing method. A few factual sentences can be enough; a new comparison tool is not required.

Closes the gap: “Can AI match a coffee to the customer’s taste preferences?”

Example of useful detail

“Choose [product A] if you prefer [supported flavor profile]. Choose [product B] if you prefer [supported alternative].”

Use verified product attributes; do not invent tasting notes.

2. Connect brewing and caffeine information to the specific product.

Clearly state the intended brewing methods and grind. Label regular and decaf options distinctly; attach any caffeine amount to its product and serving size.

Closes the gaps: Brewing-method fit and caffeine preferences.

Where to put it

On the relevant product page or a clearly linked brewing guide. A dedicated FAQ page is optional; the answer simply needs to be clear and available.

3. Provide the facts needed for a fair value comparison.

Make pack sizes and purchase prices easy to connect to the product. State a preparation amount or approximate serving yield if available. A calculated cost per serving is optional, not a required website feature.

Closes the gap: “Can AI compare value when the customer asks for an affordable coffee?” Prices vary by seller; do not promise a universal price.

How we judge the answers

We use the same clear, concise and complete standard across the coffee checklist. Finding a product name is not enough: the website must supply the facts needed for that particular recommendation.

View the assessment rules
  • Enough detail: The relevant facts are clear, concise and complete enough to support this decision.
  • Some detail missing: Information exists, but vague wording, scattered facts or missing details would require inference.
  • Not enough information: The selected readable pages do not establish enough relevant facts for this decision.
  • Could not assess: Pages or evidence were unavailable; this is not counted as a missing answer.
  • Not applicable: The question does not apply to this business or product; it is excluded from the applicable-question count.

Certification, dietary, ingredient or other requirements are added when relevant to the selected products and buying needs. This is not an exhaustive product-safety or labeling audit.
